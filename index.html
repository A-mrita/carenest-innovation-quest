01.
PEOPLE FIRST

Empathise ♡

Before thinking about features or technology, we explored the people at the heart of caregiving: caregivers and the people who depend on their support.

Persona mapping

We mapped Ananya’s caregiving responsibilities, goals, and constraints. Work limits how much time she can spend with her mother during the day, and she relies on phone alarms, notepads, and manual reminders to stay organised.

Ananya's world is split between two responsibilities.
  • She wants her mother to recover and to spend meaningful time with her.
  • Work commitments make it difficult to be physically present throughout the day.
  • She currently relies on phone alarms, notepads and manual reminders.
Takeaway: Caregiving is not one task. It is a continuous responsibility that has to fit around the caregiver's own life.
Time pressureMental loadNeed for coordination

Empathy mapping

We looked at what Ananya says, thinks, does and feels to understand the experience beyond the visible tasks.

The practical workload is connected to an emotional workload.
  • Says: remembering medicines and appointments is difficult.
  • Thinks: worries about missed medicines, emergencies and whether she is providing enough care.
  • Does: sets alarms, maintains records and books appointments.
  • Feels: overwhelmed and exhausted, but relieved when the patient is doing well.
Takeaway: A useful solution should reduce the effort of remembering and organising care, while helping the caregiver feel more reassured.
Emotional reassuranceReliable remindersOne organised place

Stakeholder analysis

We mapped the people and organisations connected to CareNest using Mendelow's Power–Interest Matrix.

Different stakeholders need different levels of involvement.
  • Manage closely: primary caregiver, development team and investors in the proposed project context.
  • Keep informed: patients, family members and competitors as placed in the team's matrix.
  • Keep satisfied / monitor: regulatory, insurance, legal, cybersecurity, healthcare and workplace-related stakeholders shown in the matrix.
Takeaway: CareNest must be easy for caregivers and patients to use, while also considering trust, privacy, healthcare context and the people who influence adoption.
User needsTrust & privacyStakeholder coordination

Journey mapping

We followed Ananya's experience across five stages and traced how her emotions change along the way.

The most difficult point is when care tasks begin to feel unmanageable.
  • Anticipate: she feels overwhelmed and worries about managing everything alone.
  • Enter / Engage: exploring and using a more organised support system brings hope and relief.
  • Exit / Reflect: she feels more reassured, organised and confident about the care being provided.
Takeaway: The opportunity is to make everyday care easier to organise and to give caregivers a clearer view of what has been done and what still needs attention.
Reduce frictionClear progressPeace of mind
CONNECTING THE DOTS

What did all these activities tell us? 💜

Across the four activities, one pattern kept appearing: caregivers are not short of care or concern. They are short of time, mental space and one reliable way to keep everyday care organised.

01 · The human need

Caregivers want to feel confident that important needs are not being missed, even when they are away.

02 · The practical gap

Medicines, appointments, records and daily requests can become scattered across alarms, notes and conversations.

03 · The design opportunity

Bring essential care information and everyday support into one simple, accessible experience for both caregiver and patient.

People & emotions→Caregiving needs→CareNest direction

How this shaped our next step: These insights guided us into the Define stage, where we narrowed the broad caregiving challenge into a clearer problem to solve.

02.
ZOOM IN ON THE NEED

Define ♡

After exploring the people and their experiences, we focused on identifying the challenge our product should address.

Problem identification

We mapped the daily tasks, emotional strain, and coordination gaps that make caregiving difficult for someone like Ananya.

Caregiving creates a coordination burden that builds across the day.
  • Daily tasks compete for attention. Appointments, follow-ups, medication routines, and meals all need tracking alongside work and personal commitments.
  • The work takes an emotional toll. Worry, guilt, and exhaustion can build when one person feels responsible for keeping everything on track.
  • Information is scattered. Keeping records up to date, sharing them with family, and finding timely support all add work.
Takeaway: Focus CareNest on making everyday care tasks and records easier to find, track, and coordinate.
Daily routinesMental loadCare coordination

Problem sizing

We considered the user segment, frequency, severity or cost, and factors that could affect the problem over time.

Problem sizing encouraged us to make our assumptions visible and think about how significant the selected problem could be.

What this stage gave us: This stage gave us a clearer direction for the solution. We moved beyond a broad topic and focused on a specific caregiving challenge.

03.
LET THE IDEAS BLOOM

Ideate ♡

With the challenge in mind, we explored different ways of thinking, generating possibilities and narrowing them towards a promising direction.

Mind mapping

We connected users, problems, needs, features, benefits and challenges around a central idea.

A mind map helped us see how user insights could connect to product ideas and possible features.

Divergent thinking

We explored multiple possibilities through brainstorming, patterns, metaphors and random association.

The goal was to widen the solution space before choosing a direction.

Convergent thinking

We compared and prioritised ideas using the 10–100–1000 gram technique.

The group weighed ideas and discussed which solution, or combination of ideas, should move forward.

What this stage gave us: The needs we explored helped us narrow many possibilities into a more focused direction for the product.

05.
A WORK IN PROGRESS

Testing ♡

A prototype is a starting point, not the finish line. Testing helps us understand what works, what feels confusing and what can be improved.

Caregiver sign-in and navigation

Sign in with an existing caregiver account and check that the Caregiver Dashboard opens. From Today’s overview, locate the medication count, the next appointment, and the most recent Care Log entry. Observe whether a first-time user can find these details without help.

View prototype screenshots

Add and find a medication record

Open Meds, select Add medication, and enter details from a fictional sample prescription. Choose Save on this device, then return to Meds and check that the record appears with the details entered. Observe whether the form labels and local save option are clear.

View prototype screenshots

Patient voice assistant

Open the Voice Assistant from the Patient Dashboard and try asking “Where can I see my medications?” first by speaking and then by typing. Check whether the assistant shows what it heard or received, whether its response makes the next step clear, and whether the patient can stop or repeat the spoken response.

View prototype screenshots

Call feature in the patient interface

From the Patient Dashboard, find Call Caregiver, then open Emergency contacts and locate the saved primary contact. Check whether the patient can open the phone’s dialler from the contact card and understands that they choose whether to place the call.

View prototype screenshots

07.
IN THE FUTURE

Changes ♡

We’ll share proposed improvements here as they take shape.

Patient linking

A caregiver enters the Patient ID shared with them to send a connection request. The patient receives an in-app notification showing the caregiver’s name and age, then chooses whether to allow the connection. Once approved, the patient’s name, ID, and illustration appear on the caregiver’s dashboard.

Scanner

Patients and caregivers could open the scanner from their own dashboards to capture a prescription. The scan would prefill a draft that they can review, correct, and save manually. Try this concept with an illustrated sample prescription and editable fields.

Multilogin

When a patient and caregiver share a device, they could keep both accounts available in CareNest. Each person would select their saved account to open the dashboard for their role, with the option to switch back from the caregiver’s More tab or the patient’s account area. The demo shows how both accounts could stay separate while making it easier to move between them.
