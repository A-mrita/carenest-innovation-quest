01
FIRST, PEOPLE

Empathise ♡

Before thinking about features or technology, we explored the people at the heart of caregiving: caregivers and the people who depend on their support.

🧍

Persona mapping

We organised a user's pains, gains, daily tasks, reality and personal context.

Meet our persona: a working daughter in her 30s who balances a full-time job with caring for her unwell mother. Her experience helped us understand the emotional, practical and time-related challenges faced by caregivers.
🫶

Empathy mapping

We considered what a user says, thinks, feels and does.

Looking at these four perspectives helps reveal needs and frustrations that may not be obvious at first.
👥

Stakeholder analysis

We identified people and groups connected to, influencing or affected by the product.

We used stakeholder mapping and a power–interest matrix to think about whose needs and involvement matter.
🗺️

Journey mapping

We explored an experience through its different touchpoints and emotional highs and lows.

Journey mapping helps identify where an experience becomes difficult and where improvements may make a difference.
💗

What this stage gave us: a people-first perspective. We kept the caregiver as the primary user and the bedridden patient as the secondary user in mind while shaping the concept.

02
ZOOM IN ON THE NEED

Define 🔎

After exploring the people and their experiences, we focused on identifying the challenge our product should address.

🎯

Problem identification

We identified a user, explored daily challenges, selected an important problem and considered possible solutions.

The activity guided us from choosing a user group to identifying a specific challenge worth addressing.
📊

Problem sizing

We considered the user segment, frequency, severity or cost, and factors that could affect the problem over time.

Problem sizing encouraged us to make our assumptions visible and think about how significant the selected problem could be.
✨

What this stage gave us: a clearer direction for the solution. It helped us move beyond a broad topic and focus on a challenge connected to caregiving.

03
LET THE IDEAS BLOOM

Ideate 💡

With the challenge in mind, we explored different ways of thinking, generating possibilities and narrowing them towards a promising direction.

🌈

Visualisation

We used a visual exercise to imagine a future vision beyond immediate limitations.

The ten-year vision activity encouraged us to communicate ideas through images, symbols, colours and visual connections.
⭕

30 Circles challenge

A timed sketching exercise to stretch creativity and generate many possibilities.

We practised quick, loose idea generation instead of judging each idea too early.
🧠

Mind mapping

We connected users, problems, needs, features, benefits and challenges around a central idea.

A mind map helped us see how user insights could connect to product ideas and possible features.
🌻

Divergent thinking

We explored multiple possibilities through brainstorming, patterns, metaphors and random association.

The goal was to widen the solution space before choosing a direction.
⚖️

Convergent thinking

We compared and prioritised ideas using the 10–100–1000 gram technique.

The group weighed ideas and discussed which solution, or combination of ideas, should move forward.
💜

What this stage gave us: a way to move from many possibilities towards a more focused product direction, guided by the needs we had explored.

04
GIVING THE IDEA A SHAPE

Prototype 🪺

We brought our selected direction into a prototype: a caregiving companion shaped around the needs we explored.

🧩

From idea to something tangible: this is where our selected concept becomes something we can show, explore and eventually improve.

A WORK IN PROGRESS · STAGE 05

Test, learn & grow 🌱

A prototype is a starting point, not the finish line. Testing helps us understand what works, what feels confusing and what can be improved.

🧪

Our next milestone

Prototype testing has not yet been completed. Our next step is to explore usability, caregiver workflows, patient assistance and opportunities for improvement with feedback from intended users.
